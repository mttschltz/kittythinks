We've talked about demand for food once the population rises, especially in sub-Saharan Africa. We've also discussed challenges such as food loss.\
Let's now turn to the question of specific resources; first up is water.

Water is present in many forms. As oceans, groundwater, ice, fresh water from rivers and lakes, permafrost, moisture in the soil and water in the atmosphere. The relationships between these entities (evaporation in the ocean, evapotranspiration from the soil and from plant matter, runoff from groundwater to the sea, and precipitation above land and oceans) form the water cycle.\
These categories can be further simplified for agricultural application into two groups:

1. **Blue Water** - which is water in lakes, streams, rivers, aquifers (water-saturated sediment), and runoff. Irrigated land is generally fed by Blue Water.
2. **Green Water** - which is water from direct precipitation. Rainfed crops are fed by Green Water.

Blue Water is therefore a supplement to Green Water, in order to modify and sustain the water cycle.  Problematically, chronic water withdrawal for irrigation is depleting freshwater resources, which is unsustainable. However, certain areas of the world _must_ improve their irrigation capabilities to improve food security.
